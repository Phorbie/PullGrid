Hardware

PullGrid prints the parts that have to fit your cabinet and buys the parts that are just straight lines. Every item below is stocked by ordinary big-box hardware stores in the US and Canada (inch sizes) and the UK, Ireland, Australia and New Zealand (metric sizes). The printed parts accept either size, so buy whatever your local store has.

The generator's Buy list gives you exact quantities and lengths for your cabinet. Set Where you'll buy it to your region and the names and sizes switch to match. For one pull-out, expect roughly US$50–70 or the local equivalent, mostly the slides and the angle. A cabinet with fixed shelves needs a set per tier.

What to look for

Slides. Get full-extension (the shelf comes all the way out), side-mount, ball-bearing slides, 45 mm tall. That's the most common kind in every market. Soft-close versions work too and are nice with a mounted door: the clearance spec is the same. Avoid undermount or epoxy-coated roller slides; they're a different shape. Look for a disconnect lever (most have one): you'll use it to unclip the pull-out during installation.

Aluminum angle. Any equal-leg L-shaped angle. The generator picks the leg size: usually 1½″ / 40 mm, or 2″ / 50 mm when your cabinet's bottom rail lifts the slides higher. Thicker is better for holding screws, but even 1.4 mm holds fine because the load is sideways. One length usually covers both rails; the Cut list says how long each piece is.

Threaded rod. Plain zinc-plated rod is fine. In the US it's sold in 2, 3 and 6 ft lengths; elsewhere usually 1 m. The Cut list gives the length of each tie rod.

Screws. Exact sizes don't matter much: any pan-head screw that fits the slide's holes works for the slides, and the printed holes take #8 and 4 mm screws.

Why these items

  • Aluminum angle instead of printed rails: the rails are just straight L-shapes. Angle is stronger, holds screws better than plastic, gives back about 10 mm of width, and cuts about a fifth of the printing. Prefer not to cut metal? Choose Printed rails in the generator.
  • Threaded rod: steel in tension clamps the split printed parts together, so those seams need no glue, and the pull-out can be taken apart later.
  • What PullGrid avoids: aluminum extrusion (2020 and friends) isn't in big-box stores and needs special nuts; M3 screws aren't reliably stocked in US stores; #4-40 screws don't exist in metric markets.

Tools

  • Tape measure
  • Hacksaw with a metal blade, and a file (for the angle and rod ends)
  • Drill, with a 1/8″ / 3 mm bit for pilot holes (slide screws into the angle and the printed cheeks) and a 3/16″ / 5 mm bit for the angle's floor holes
  • Screwdriver (a long one helps reach the lower door brackets)
  • Wrench or socket: 7/16″ for ¼″ nuts, 10 mm for M6
  • Masking tape and a pencil for marking
